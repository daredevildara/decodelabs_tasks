# Project 3 — SQL Data Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project focuses on using SQL to analyse a transactional sales dataset and extract meaningful business insights from the data.

The analysis was carried out using SQLite, with SQL queries designed to examine sales performance, customer behaviour, payment preferences, marketing performance, order statuses, and changes in sales activity over time.

Rather than focusing only on individual transactions, the project uses aggregation, grouping, filtering, sorting, and calculated metrics to identify broader patterns within the dataset.

The analysis is structured around seven key areas:

1. Dataset Overview
2. Product Performance
3. Customer & Order Behaviour
4. Payment Behaviour
5. Marketing Performance
6. Order-Status Analysis
7. Time-Based Analysis

The results of each analysis were documented alongside query outputs and interpreted from a business perspective.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The main objectives of this project were to:

- Use SQL to explore and analyse a structured sales dataset.
- Apply SQL aggregation functions such as COUNT(), SUM(), and AVG().
- Use GROUP BY to compare categories and identify patterns.
- Use ORDER BY to rank results based on relevant business metrics.
- Apply conditional logic using CASE.
- Analyse customer purchasing behaviour.
- Compare product and payment-method performance.
- Evaluate referral-source and coupon activity.
- Examine the distribution of orders across different statuses.
- Identify stronger and weaker periods of sales performance.
- Translate SQL query results into clear, business-focused insights.

---

# 🔍 Analysis & Key Findings

## 1. Dataset Overview

### Business Question

What is the overall commercial scale and coverage of the dataset?

### SQL Concepts Used

- COUNT()
- COUNT(DISTINCT)
- MIN()
- MAX()
- SUM()
- AVG()

### Key Findings

The dataset contains 1,200 orders generated by 1,189 unique customers, covering the period from 1 January 2023 to 30 June 2025.

The recorded orders represent a total sales value of 1,264,761.96, with an average order value of approximately 1,053.97.

These figures establish the overall scale, customer coverage, and time range used as the baseline for the subsequent analyses.

---

## 2. Product Performance

### Business Question

How do products differ in demand, sales contribution, and order value? 

### SQL Concepts Used

- GROUP BY
- SUM()
- AVG()
- COUNT()
- ORDER BY

### Key Findings

Chair recorded the highest total sales value among the products analysed at 195,620.11.

Printer recorded the highest order volume, with 181 orders, indicating the greatest transaction frequency among the products.

Laptop recorded the highest average order value at approximately 1,110.56, showing that its individual orders tended to have a higher recorded value.

At the other end of the ranking, Phone recorded the lowest order volume, with 156 orders, and the lowest average order value at approximately 972.58.

The results demonstrate that product performance can vary depending on the metric used. The product generating the greatest total sales is not necessarily the product with the highest order frequency or highest average transaction value.

---

## 3. Customer & Order Behaviour

### Business Question

How frequently do customer place orders, and what does the customer base look like in term of purchasing frequency? 

### SQL Concepts Used

- Subqueries
- GROUP BY
- CASE
- COUNT()
- SUM()
- Calculated metrics

### Key Findings

The customer base is overwhelmingly composed of one-time purchasers.

Of the 1,189 unique customers, 1,178 were one-time customers, while only 11 customers placed more than one order.

One-time customers accounted for 1,178 orders and generated 1,245,226.18 in recorded sales.

Repeat customers accounted for 22 orders and generated 19,535.78 in recorded sales, with an average order value of approximately 887.99.

The results therefore show that sales activity is heavily concentrated among one-time purchases.

From a business perspective, this pattern could support further investigation into customer retention and repeat-purchase opportunities. However, the SQL results alone do not establish why repeat purchasing is limited.

---

## 4. Payment Behaviour

### Business Question

How does order value and activity differ across payment methods?

### SQL Concepts Used

- GROUP BY
- COUNT()
- SUM()
- AVG()
- ORDER BY

### Key Findings

Payment activity is relatively evenly distributed across the five payment methods.

Online recorded the highest order volume, with 258 orders, followed by Cash with 246 orders and Credit Card with 234 orders.

Despite having fewer transactions than Online and Cash, Credit Card generated the highest total sales value at 263,847.63 and also recorded the highest average order value at approximately 1,127.56.

Debit Card recorded the lowest average order value at approximately 1,001.57.

The results show that the most frequently used payment method is not necessarily the method associated with the greatest sales value or transaction value.

---

## 5. Marketing Performance

### Business Question

Which acquisition sources attract the most valuable order activity, and how is coupon usage distributed across them?

This section examines both overall referral-source performance and the relationship between referral sources and coupon usage.

---

### 5.1 Referral Source Performance

#### Key Findings

Instagram was the strongest acquisition source overall, recording 259 orders and the highest total sales value at approximately 275,285.45.

Email followed with 250 orders and approximately 261,808.65 in recorded sales, while Google generated 241 orders and approximately 250,441.48.

Facebook recorded fewer orders than Email and Google but had a relatively high average order value of approximately 1,098.29, the highest among the referral sources.

Referral recorded the lowest overall activity, with 222 orders and approximately 226,815.68 in sales.

The results demonstrate that acquisition volume, total sales, and average transaction value can highlight different aspects of marketing performance.

---

### 5.2 Referral Source & Coupon Performance

#### Key Findings

Coupon usage varied across acquisition sources.

For Instagram, FREESHIP was the most frequently associated coupon, appearing across 73 orders.

For Email, FREESHIP was also the largest coupon category, with 68 orders.

For Facebook, the largest category was No Coupon, with 67 orders.

For Google, SAVE10 and No Coupon each appeared across 66 orders.

For Referral, No Coupon was the largest category, with 60 orders.

At the referral-source and coupon level, Instagram + FREESHIP produced the highest total sales value at 78,536.58 from 73 orders.

This was followed by Facebook + No Coupon, with 76,459.75 from 67 orders, and Google + SAVE10, with 74,738.86 from 66 orders.

Email + No Coupon recorded the highest average order value at approximately 1,210.43.

These results describe observed associations between acquisition sources, coupon usage, order volume, and sales value. They do not establish that a particular coupon caused higher sales.

---

## 6. Order-Status Analysis

### Business Question

How does order status affect the distribution of order activity and sales value, and where is the sales activity potentially being lost or left unresolved? 

### SQL Concepts Used

- GROUP BY
- COUNT()
- SUM()
- AVG()
- ORDER BY

### Key Findings

Order activity is relatively evenly distributed across the five statuses, with no single status accounting for a dominant share.

Cancelled orders recorded the highest volume at 250 orders and the highest total recorded sales value at 276,396.21, with an average order value of approximately 1,105.68.

Pending orders followed with 237 orders and 256,328.15 in recorded sales.

Shipped accounted for 235 orders and 246,159.58 in recorded sales.

Returned orders accounted for 247 orders and approximately 243,277.70 in recorded sales.

Delivered orders recorded 231 orders and 242,600.32 in recorded sales, with an average order value of approximately 1,050.22.

The results highlight a substantial amount of recorded order value associated with cancelled, pending, and returned orders rather than delivered orders alone.

However, these figures should be interpreted as recorded order value, not automatically as realised or lost revenue, because the dataset does not establish whether cancelled or returned orders were refunded.

The analysis therefore provides an operational perspective in which fulfilment outcomes should be considered alongside sales activity when assessing overall business performance.

## 7. Time-Based Analysis

### Business Question

When does the business experience its strongest and weakest periods of order activity and sales performance?

### SQL Concepts Used

- strftime()
- GROUP BY
- COUNT()
- SUM()
- AVG()
- ORDER BY
- LIMIT

### Strongest-Performing Months

| Month | Orders | Total Sales | Average Order Value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| June 2024 | 53 | 68,068.54 | 1,284.31 |
| May 2023 | 49 | 63,836.84 | 1,302.79 |
| January 2023 | 47 | 56,865.75 | 1,209.90 |
| August 2023 | 51 | 54,352.14 | 1,065.73 |
| June 2025 | 49 | 53,047.40 | 1,082.60 |

### Key Findings

June 2024 was the strongest-performing month, generating 68,068.54 in recorded sales from 53 orders, with an average order value of approximately 1,284.31.

May 2023 followed with 63,836.84 in sales from 49 orders and an average order value of approximately 1,302.79.

The results show that strong monthly performance can be driven by both transaction volume and transaction value.

---

### Weakest-Performing Months

| Month | Orders | Total Sales | Average Order Value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| April 2023 | 31 | 27,751.71 | 895.22 |
| August 2024 | 34 | 27,909.11 | 820.86 |
| January 2025 | 27 | 29,099.40 | 1,077.76 |
| September 2023 | 29 | 29,526.67 | 1,018.16 |
| April 2025 | 32 | 31,821.20 | 994.41 |

### Key Findings

April 2023 recorded the lowest sales value at 27,751.71 from 31 orders, followed by August 2024 at 27,909.11 from 34 orders.

January 2025 generated 29,099.40 from only 27 orders, demonstrating that lower order volume does not necessarily correspond to the lowest sales value.

Overall, the time-based results reveal substantial month-to-month variation and reinforce the importance of considering both transaction volume and transaction value.

# 💡 Overall Insights

The analysis produced several broad observations from the dataset:

- Sales activity is distributed across multiple products, payment methods, referral sources, and order statuses rather than being concentrated in a single category.
- Product performance differs depending on whether performance is measured by total sales, order volume, or average order value.
- The customer base is overwhelmingly composed of one-time purchasers, with very limited repeat purchasing activity.
- Credit Card transactions produced the highest total sales value and average order value despite not having the highest transaction volume.
- Instagram generated the highest overall referral-source sales value and order volume.
- Coupon usage varied by referral source, with different source-and-coupon combinations producing different sales outcomes.
- A large amount of recorded order value is associated with non-delivered statuses, although the dataset does not provide enough information to determine the financial outcome of cancelled or returned orders.
- Monthly sales performance varied considerably, demonstrating that transaction volume alone does not fully explain sales value.

# Conclusion

This project demonstrates how SQL can be used to move from raw transactional data to structured business analysis.

By combining aggregation, grouping, conditional logic, ranking, and time-based analysis, the project examines the dataset from multiple perspectives rather than relying on a single performance metric.

The analysis also highlights the importance of interpreting SQL results within context. Metrics such as total sales, order volume, average order value, customer frequency, and order status can tell different parts of the same business story.

Overall, the project strengthened practical SQL skills while demonstrating how query results can be translated into clear, evidence-based business insights.
